---

#### Lining Fabric

Lightweight but sturdy fabrics. You want a fabric that is not going to add a lot of bulk but is sturdy enough to hold things. I like lightweight cottons of shirting weight such as **Cotton Lawn**. In testing this design I actually recycled a shirt for the pocket fabric.

#### Interfacing

I personally like to use calico but you can use whatever sew-on or iron-on interfacing you prefer.

## Callum casual shorts: Sewing Instructions
---


### Step 1: Constructing the back and back pockets

- Close the back darts.
- Mark the stitching lines of the pocket opening on the *good side* of the right back leg. (I recommend 1 / 2" (12mm) wide rectangle, 1 / 4" (6mm) each side of the pocket opening line but it is up to personal prefence.)
- (Optional) Interface the back pocket welts.
- Press under or *Finsih* press under the long edges of the back pocket welts.

<Note> You may not want to interface the seam allowance of the long edges if you are pressing under. </Note>

- Sew the back pocket welts to the back pocket bag *wrong sides* to *good sides* along the edges making sure to match the pocket opening lines.
- Mark the stitching lines of the pocket opening on the *wrong side* of the back pocket bag, you only need to mark one of them. (This needs to be the same as the markings on the back leg)
- Pin the back pocket bag to the right back pocket leg *good sides together* making sure to match pocket opening stitching lines.
- Sew along the stitching lines to form the opening rectangle.
- Along the pocket opening line on boths sides mark the point that is the half the length of the rectangle width in.
- Through all layers cut along the pocket opening line between these two marks the from these marks clip to each corner of the opening rectangle. Be careful not to cut outside of the opening rectangle.
- Turn the pocket bag through the opening so the pocket is on the *wrong side* of the back leg. Press the seams flat so that the opening is clear.
- *Finish* the top and bottom edges of the opening rectangle.

#### For single welt

- Fold up so the folded edege meets the top of the pocket opening. Press!

#### For double welt

- On the *wrong side* of the pocket bag mark halfway of the pocket opening on the short sides.
- Fold the pocket so that a fold edge meets the halfway mark from the bottom and the top. Use pins if you need to hold in place before pressing. Press!

#### Continue for both

- Make sure everything is flat and you are happy with how the opening looks before proceding to the next steps.
- Fold back the back leg so you have the little triangle on top. Sew through all layers at the base of the little triangle to secure the welt. **Do not sew it** to the back leg.
- Repeat for the bottom edge of the welt pocket.
- If you wish the secure the top edge through only one layer of the pocket bag repeat the above for the top edge of the welt pocket.
- On the *good sides*, *bartack* the short sides of the welt pocket opening either close to or on the edge. (If you wish the bartacking to secure both layers of the pocket bag you will need to this step after constructing the pocket bag and securing the top edge of the opening)
- If you wish to *edge stitch* the bottom edge of the pocket opening now is the time to do so.


<Note>

I will now explain to make the welt pocket with a french seam. If you prefer to make without a french sew the pocket bag together like a regular seam *good sides together*, *finishing* the edges.

</Note>

- Pull the pocket bag back through the opeing.
- Fold the back pocket bag *wrong sides together* and sew together along the sides with half your seam allownance (by default this is a 1 / 4" (6mm)). Trim the seam to at the very most 1 / 8" (3mm).
	- If you have two pocket bags too sew together you will need to sew the bottom as well creating a u-shape of stitching.
	- If you do not wish the top of the pocket bag to sit in the waistband you will need to trim down the top of the pocket bag and also sew it creating either a n-shape or o-shape of stitching.
- Turn the pocket bag through the opening so the pocket is on the *wrong side* of the back leg.
- Before continuing make sure to re-allign the pocket opening/welt if it has move. Press.
- Fold down the back leg so the top of the opening rectangle is on top. Sew through all layers at the base of the top of the opening rectangle to secure the welt. **Do not sew it** to the back leg.
- _Baste_ the top of the pocket bag to the top of the back leg so it lies flat. The design of the pocket bag is built to overhang the top of the back leg to make this easier and the excess can be trimmed.

<Note>

The next set of instructions is how to finish the cross seam with a flat fell using the default seam allowance set by the design. If you have altered the seam allowance or just want to sew the cross seam differently then you should sew and _finish_ the cross seam in your preferred method before moving onto the front.

</Note>

- With *wrong sides together*, sew the back legs together along the cross seam. Press open.
- Trim down the left leg's cross seam seam allowance to no more that 1/8" (3mm).
- Press the right legs cross seam allowance to the left then fold under the right seam allowance. Press. Pin if you need too.
- _Edgestitch_ the fold in place.
