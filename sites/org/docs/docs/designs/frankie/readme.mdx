## What You Need {#needs}

To make Frankie, you will need the following:

- Basic sewing supplies
- About 2 metres (2.2 yards) of main fabric ([see Frankie Fabric Options](#fabric))
- About 0.5 metres (0.6 yards) of lining fabric ([see Frankie Fabric Options](#fabric))
- 1 closed end zipper or buttons for the placket
- 1 button or hook and bar/eye for the waistband
- (If needed) Interfacing for waistband and placket

## Fabric Options {#fabric}

### Main Fabric

Frankie can be made from a wide range of fabrics but you want to be careful about matching the fabric to the fullness of the legs.

- **Cotton**

A very good choice for beginners that allows Frankie to be made in a variety of weights and weaves.
We recommend going for **Twill**, **Chambray** or **Denim** to really help keep the flares from collapsing but for a pop of colour you can go with **Printed Cottons** of **Poplin** & **Calico** types.
If you're Frankie is very full you may want to consider a light weight cotton such as **Lawns**, just bear in mind the flare will hang down more than out.

:::comment

250g/sqm twill is pretty much perfect. Not too light, not too heavy, gives just the right amount of swoosh without the flare completely collapsing. It's not like heavier fabrics won't work, but this is great.

:::

- **Wool**

Wool is a great choice for Frankie especially if you are planning to wear Frankie in colder climates.
However due to the fullness of Frankie you may want to keep to **light** and **medium weight** wools.
**Checks** and **Herringbones** can give a classic/vintage feel to Frankie, especially if your Frankie is drafted more like bell bottoms.
You may find your wool requires you to fully line Frankie to avoid sensory issues with scratchy wools.

- **Linen**

Linen is a classic choice and fun to work with. It is a great for hotter climates.
Bear in mind linen tends to wrinkle so if pressing your garments is not your thing go for a **Twill**.

### Lining (Front Pocket) Fabric

Basic **Cotton** will do the trick for the pocket bags. If you've got left over
cotton from another project you can easily use it up for these pockets.
Otherwise any fabric you have that you deem suitable for pockets will do.

## Cutting Instructions {#cutting}

**Main fabric**

- Cut **2 back** parts
- Cut **2 front** parts
- Cut **1 fly shield** part on fold
- If zipper Cut **2 fly** parts
- If buttonhole Cut **1 fly** part
- If buttonhole Cut **1 buttonhole placket** part on fold
- If curved or unfolded Cut **2 waistband** parts
- If straight & folded Cut **1 waistband** part
- (Optional) Cut **4 front pocket** facings
- (Optional) Cut **2 side pocket** parts
- (Optional) Cut **1 belt loop** part

**Lining fabric**

- Cut **4 front pocket bag** parts

:::tip

Frankie can have very wide legs so do not be afraid to _piece_ the front and back parts rather than cutting them in one piece.
Do not forget to sew these seams first before starting construction.

:::

:::note

The front pocket facings will need to be constructed off of the front pocket bag part.

:::
